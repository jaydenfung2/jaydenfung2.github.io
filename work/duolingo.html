Priced on negative sentiment, not fundamentals.

Duolingo is a profitable learning leader with sticky subscribers and strong growth, priced at mature-consumer multiples. AI concerns and a pivot from monetisation to daily-user growth spooked the market, leaving it near all-time lows at 11.75x trailing and 12.35x forward earnings, against FY25 revenue growth of 39%.

Two arguments.

The moat is behavioural, and AI can't replace it.

Others build features. Duolingo builds habits: bite-sized lessons, streaks, reminders and leaderboards, with friends and leagues adding social accountability. 70% of users are driven by notifications and daily streaks. Between September 2024 and October 2025, users on a 90+ day streak rose from 22% to 26%, and those under 7 days fell from 19% to 14%. With 50M+ daily users, Duolingo can test tiny changes with statistical significance, and those retention gains compound.

Multiple compression came from sentiment, not deterioration.

The AI translation argument misreads why people use the product. As CEO Luis von Ahn said on the Q4 2025 earnings call, translation between large languages has been essentially perfect for over ten years, and users learn as a hobby or because they want to learn English. The wider SaaS sell-off is about agents disrupting predictable subscription revenue, yet agents don't interact with Duolingo. It was simply brought down with the industry.

Risks and catalysts.

Guidance conservatism

Cautious guidance may anchor expectations to sub-20% bookings growth, but management has a history of beating initial guidance.

Macro headwinds

Softer discretionary spending could limit short-term ARPU expansion, but the freemium model, low price point and habit mechanics protect retention.

Catalysts: Q1 and Q2 bookings guidance beats, engagement from Chess and Maths, international expansion including Max approval in China, sentiment recovery, and growth in Duolingo English Test revenue.
